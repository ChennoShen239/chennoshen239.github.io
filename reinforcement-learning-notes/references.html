References

Main sources

Ben Moll, Reinforcement Learning for Economists, lecture slides, London School of Economics. https://benjaminmoll.com/lectures/

: The main source for Chapters 1 to 4.

Shiyu Zhao, Mathematical Foundations of Reinforcement Learning, Springer, 2025. https://github.com/MathFoundationRL/Book-Mathematical-Foundation-of-Reinforcement-Learning

: Moll describes his slides as “mostly a shortened/adapted version” of Zhao’s book and refers to it for omitted proofs. It supplies the proofs developed here: Dvoretzky’s theorem and its multi-variable form (Chapter 6 there), the convergence of TD, Sarsa, and Q-learning and the action-value Bellman equations (Chapter 7), and the policy gradient theorem (Chapter 9). The full book and individual chapter PDFs are free on the repository above, which also carries the grid-world code the lectures point at.

Cited along the way

Richard S. Sutton and Andrew G. Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed., MIT Press, 2018. http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html

: The standard reference. Cited here for the constant-\(\alpha\) Monte Carlo comparison in Chapter 1, for the incremental-versus-episodic distinction in Chapter 3 (where this book departs from its “online/offline” vocabulary, since “offline RL” now means something else), and for the REINFORCE pseudocode in Chapter 4.

David Silver, Guy Lever, Nicolas Heess, Thomas Degris, Daan Wierstra, and Martin Riedmiller, “Deterministic Policy Gradient Algorithms”, ICML, 2014.

The deterministic policy gradient theorem of Chapter 4.

Richard S. Sutton, David McAllester, Satinder Singh, and Yishay Mansour, “Policy Gradient Methods for Reinforcement Learning with Function Approximation”, NeurIPS, 2000.

The stochastic policy gradient theorem of Chapter 4.

Kevin P. Murphy, Reinforcement Learning: An Overview, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05265

: A survey cited in Chapter 4 for its treatment of actor-critic methods; it is less accessible than Zhao as an introduction.

State Abstraction

Lihong Li, Thomas J. Walsh, and Michael L. Littman, “Towards a Unified Theory of State Abstraction for MDPs,” International Symposium on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, 2006. https://thomasjwalsh.net/pub/aima06Towards.pdf

: Chapter 5 follows Definition 1, the preservation conditions in Definition 3, and the results on planning and Q-learning in Theorems 3–4.

Per Krusell and Anthony A. Smith, Jr., “Income and Wealth Heterogeneity in the Macroeconomy,” Journal of Political Economy 106(5), 867–896, 1998. Journal article · Full text

: The benchmark household problem, equilibrium, and forecast evidence in Chapter 5.

Wouter J. den Haan, “Assessing the Accuracy of the Aggregate Law of Motion in Models with Heterogeneous Agents,” Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control 34(1), 79–99, 2010. Journal article · Author manuscript

: The recursive forecast check and the example on capital volatility in Chapter 5.

Further reading the lectures recommend

Lilian Weng, “Policy Gradient Algorithms”, 2018. https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2018-04-08-policy-gradient/

: A survey of policy gradient algorithms beyond those covered in Chapter 4.

Andrej Karpathy, “Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT”, 2025. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7xTGNNLPyMI

: Applications of Chapter 4: RLHF, RLVR, and reasoning models.

John Schulman, Filip Wolski, Prafulla Dhariwal, Alec Radford, and Oleg Klimov, “Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms”, 2017. https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347

: The source for Chapter 4’s description of PPO’s clipped surrogate.

Topics beyond the lectures

Zhao covers further topics: \(n\)-step Sarsa and TD(\(\lambda\)), off-policy learning with importance sampling, Deep Q-learning with experience replay and target networks, the convergence theory of function approximation, and the wider actor-critic family (A2C, DPG, TRPO, PPO).
